Topic: the spotlight effect

EXAMPLE SCRIPT: STYLE GUIDE ONLY.
Never use this example's topic, facts, numbers, examples or sentences in your script.
Your script uses ONLY the research for YOUR topic. Learn only HOW it is written:
- the hook creates curiosity in one short line
- the body is built on ONE core fact with a real study and real numbers (copied exactly from research)
- the study is described as something other people did, never as something the viewer did
- one everyday moment the viewer instantly recognizes
- light humor only where it fits naturally, from a specific detail
- short spoken sentences, no filler, no repeated ideas
- the ending gives the viewer a useful new way to see their own life

Hook 1: You think everyone noticed. Science says almost nobody did.
Hook 2: Your most embarrassing moment is far less memorable than you think.
Hook 3: Why does it feel like the whole room is watching you?
Body: In 2000, psychologists at Cornell asked students to walk into a room wearing a T-shirt with a giant
picture of the singer Barry Manilow. The students were sure everyone would notice. They guessed about half
the room saw the shirt. The real number was about one in four. Psychologists call this the spotlight effect.
We feel like we stand under a bright stage light, because we see every detail of our own moment. Everyone
else is busy with their own thoughts, their own phone, and their own questionable shirt. It works for good
moments too. That clever line you said in the meeting barely registered with most people. So the next time
you trip on the stairs, remember that you are probably the only one still replaying it.
CTA: Follow for more psychology that makes everyday life make sense.
